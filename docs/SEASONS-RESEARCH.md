# Seasons research: how race calendars and training arcs line up with the year

Research for the season model. Marathon and HYROX are the sports we build for first (D-050); the IRONMAN section is kept for when triathlon is added. Gathered 2026-10-28. Kevin decides the model; this doc gives him the facts and a recommendation.

## 1. Summary

- **Race days fit thirds well. Training builds don't.** A 16-week build fits inside one third on only 29 race days a year (8%). An 18-week marathon build or a 24-week Ironman build never does. Any season-only model splits the athlete's biggest story in two.
- **Spring builds start the year before.** Tokyo, Boston and London builds start in November or December, so the January–April story begins in last year's fall season, and training through the holidays sits in the middle of it.
- **The sports run their own seasons, and none of them follow the calendar year.** The HYROX season runs from about July to a mid-May qualifying cutoff, with Worlds in June. Kona qualifying runs from August to the following September.
- **Decision (D-049):** seasons belong to the athlete. We build a default season plan from the races they're training for, and they can edit it. Thirds are the fallback for athletes with no races. A fixed calendar underneath handles comparisons. Sport seasons (HYROX 26/27, the Kona qualifying year) are labels only.

## 2. The marathon majors

There are seven Majors now, not five or six: Sydney joined in 2025. Cape Town becomes the eighth with its 2027 race.

| Tokyo | Mar 1 | Mar 7 | S1 |
| Boston | Apr 20 | Apr 19 | S1 |
| London | Apr 26 | Apr 24–25 (two-day format rumored, not announced) | S1 |
| Cape Town | May 24 (assessment year) | May 23 (first as a Major) | S2 |
| Sydney | Aug 30 | Aug 29 | S2 |
| Berlin | Sep 27 | Sep 26 | S3 |
| Chicago | Oct 11 | Oct 10 | S3 |
| New York City | Nov 1 | Nov 7 | S3 |

In the US, fall is the biggest marathon season. October has the most races and the most finishers, and an older Running USA-era summary put more than half of all marathon finishes in Oct–Dec. Spring (April especially) comes second. These sources are secondary and of mixed age; RunSignup or Running USA data would confirm the ranking.

**How runners train:** common amateur plans run 16–20 weeks: base, build (threshold and marathon pace), peak, then a 2–3 week taper. Runners who already have a base use 12-week plans. Two to four easy weeks follow the race. These numbers are coaching convention, not research findings.

## 3. HYROX

- **The season runs across years and is labeled that way** (season 26/27). Events start in late July (Delhi, July 24–26, 2026, by one calendar; Red Bull credits Gdańsk in October as the opener). The Elite Series starts later (Hamburg, Oct 30–31, 2026).
- **Qualifying cutoff:** events up to the weekend of May 16, 2027 hold slots for the 2027 World Championships. Events after that count toward 2028.
- **World Championships:** Stockholm, June 18–21, 2026; Hong Kong, June 10–13, 2027.
- **Qualifying:** at least one slot per age group in every Pro and Doubles Mixed division at every event. About 0.5% of competitors are expected to qualify.
- **How athletes train:** prep blocks run 6–8 weeks (advanced), 8–10 (intermediate) or 10–12+ (beginner), 3–5 sessions a week. Gym programs run continuous blocks; Club Hybrid uses 8-week blocks (base, threshold, race-specific, maintenance). Amateurs race several times a season, so HYROX is a long competitive season made of short campaigns, unlike a marathoner's one or two A-races.

The HYROX year turns over in May–June, inside S2. S2 becomes the transition season: cutoff, Worlds, summer lull, then the next season opens.

## 4. IRONMAN

- **World Championship:** Kona, October 10, 2026, back to one day with men and women racing together (2023–2025 split them between Kona and Nice). The 2027 date wasn't in sources found; it has historically been in October.
- **70.3 World Championship:** Nice, September 12–13, 2026.
- **Qualifying:** only full-distance races count for Kona. For 2027, 34 qualifying events run from August 2026 to September 2027. The age-group winner gets an automatic slot; the rest come from a performance pool measured against an age-graded "Kona Standard." Age is taken as of December 31 of the race year.
- **North American full-distance calendar, 2027:** Texas (Apr 24), Jacksonville (May 16), Lake Placid (Jul 25), Canada/Ottawa (Aug 8), Montana (Aug 15), Wisconsin (Sep 12), Maryland (Sep 18), Chattanooga (Sep 26), California (Oct 24), Florida/Panama City Beach (Nov 6). Racing runs April to early November, heaviest in late summer and fall.
- **How athletes train:** plans run 20–30 weeks, most often 24: about 8 weeks each of base, build and peak, a recovery week every fourth week, and a 2-week taper. 70.3 plans are commonly 20–24 weeks. Age-groupers usually build to one or two A-races a year.

## 6. What thirds get right

- **Races land in clear seasons.** Spring marathons close S1 (Boston and London fall in its last two weeks). S2 holds summer triathlon, Sydney and HYROX Worlds. S3 holds the fall marathons and Kona.
- **S1 ends on the spring A-race.** For a spring marathoner, the season story ends at the finish line, the shape a season should have.
- **S3 ends after the races.** The fall marathons and Kona finish by mid-November, so the last six weeks of S3 are recovery and holidays. That's a real pattern; the engine has to treat it as the off-season, not a decline.

## 7. The model (D-049)

Section 5 shows why a fixed calendar can't be the story frame. So the athlete's own races set the seasons, and we only supply the default.

**Athlete seasons (the story frame, editable)**

- **Input:** the races the athlete is training for. They enter them, or confirm ones we found in a registration or result. Each race is tagged A, B or C. Onboarding asks "What are you training for?", and "Nothing yet" is a fine answer.
- **Each A race makes a season.** Start: race day minus the sport's default build (marathon 18 weeks, HYROX 10; later, 70.3 20 and Ironman 24). When the gym runs fixed blocks (Club Hybrid's 8 weeks), snap the start to a block edge. End: 3 weeks after race day. The default name comes from the race ("Chicago 2027").
- **B and C races** are milestones inside a season, not seasons.
- **No overlaps.** A later season starts when the earlier one's recovery ends. Its build comes out shorter than the default, and we show that.
- **Merge close races.** A races less than 10 weeks apart become one season ending at the last race. This keeps a HYROX athlete racing every 6–8 weeks from getting a pile of tiny seasons.
- **Gaps** between seasons are off-seasons, named that way.
- **No races:** thirds (Jan–Apr, May–Aug, Sep–Dec).
- **The athlete can rename, move or delete any season.** Build lengths are defaults labeled as estimates, as with class block boundaries (D-038).

**Fixed calendar (underneath, never edited)**

Monday-start weeks, months and years in the athlete's time zone. Used for grouping, year-over-year comparison and as the frame when there's no season. Seasons of different lengths are compared on per-week rates, or week N of this build against week N of the last.

**Sport seasons (labels only)**

HYROX 26/27, the Kona 2027 qualifying year. Context only ("your third race of the 26/27 season"), taken from fixtures or federation data.

**Rules that follow from the non-negotiables**

- **Stories store their exact date range,** not just a season ID, so later edits to a season don't change approved stories.
- **A planned race is the athlete's plan, not a fact.** "12 weeks to Chicago" is fine. A finish-time projection only appears if code computed it, labeled as a projection.
- **A race that isn't run** (skipped, cancelled, DNF) still ends the season. The story can say the race wasn't run, but it never gives a reason the athlete didn't give.
- **Season and race names are athlete-typed text,** so they fall under the imported-text rule: shown and quoted, never followed as instructions.
- **Followers see seasons and names only after the athlete approves them.**
