## 1. What the HYROX page does

### Three registers on one page

The page runs to about 8,000 words, and HYROX's own marketing voice accounts for about 1,200 of them: the hero, the coaches, the four benefit cards, the programme tabs and the training concept. The rest splits between the cruise line's brochure copy (cabins, dining, the ship) and plain logistics (check-in, fees, cancellation, the FAQ).

| Register | What it sounds like | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing** | High energy, imperative verbs, the reader cast as an athlete | The part worth studying |
| **Brochure** | Spa, sunsets and ocean views that would fit any cruise | The weakest copy on the page |
| **Logistics** | Exact times, fees in euros, one capitalised warning for the 18:00 check-in cutoff | Good. It reads like race-day athlete info |

The lesson: the voice changes with the job. When the reader has to act correctly (check in by 18:00, pay 65% thirty days out), the page drops the style and gives instructions.

### Nine moves that work

1. **It calls the reader an athlete and puts them next to the pros.** You train with Elite 15 racers. The programme comes from HYROX's head of training, who designed the HYROX365 method and raced five Elite seasons. The promise is access to how the top of the sport works. That is OpenHealth's thesis too: pro treatment for amateurs.
2. **Progress language runs through it.** Smarter, harder, faster, better, sharper, deeper. Every benefit points at the reader's next HYROX race. This audience lives on a race calendar.
3. **It speaks the sport without translating.** Ergs, AMRAPs, threshold training, race-prep, SkiErg, RowErg, Elite 15. No glossary. Fluency signals membership; a glossary would signal an outsider talking.
4. **Serious and social sit side by side.** Training sections share the page with live DJs, sunset sessions and parties. "Community" appears 8 times in 1,200 words. Hybrid athletes train in classes and race in waves with friends, so the people are half the product.
5. **One lens covers everything.** The cabin becomes a recovery zone. Dining becomes fuel. Nothing on the page escapes the performance frame, and that consistency makes the whole page feel like one idea.
6. **The structure looks like a training plan.** Four pillars, four decks, seven stations, four scored challenges, each with a one-word name (Core, Oxygen, Pulse, Breath, Drive). This audience likes programming. A named taxonomy reads like a programme, and short names are easy to say out loud.
7. **Specifics carry the proof.** 350+ hours of sessions, 150+ athletes per GroupX class, 7 stations, 4 decks, 4 days, a named head coach with credentials. Numbers do the convincing; adjectives only set the mood.
8. **The rhythm alternates long and short.** A long sentence lists what you get, then a run of two-word imperatives lands it. The tagline is the clearest case: "Train smarter. Learn faster. Race better." The route section counts down four days, three stops, one experience in fragments. It reads like a coach running a session: explain the workout, then call short cues.
9. **It names both ends of the ladder.** The copy speaks to the athlete brand new to HYROX and the one chasing a podium in the same sentence. Nobody feels too slow to belong, and nobody feels it's beneath them.

### What doesn't work (don't copy)

- **Superlative inflation.** In 1,200 words: unique ×4, unforgettable ×4, immersive ×4, plus iconic, exclusive, first-of-its-kind and never-seen-before, plus a "most" in front of unique. Each one cancels the last. The Playbook already bans unscoped superlatives; our version scopes them ("fastest of your four HYROX races").
- **Claims nobody can check.** Praise for the coaches as the most respected and the very best sits next to their real credentials. The credentials do the work; the claims add nothing.
- **Brochure filler.** The ship paragraphs could describe any cruise on any sea.
- **Careless errors.** A doubled word, a missing word in one sentence, "an" before a consonant sound, steps numbered 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 6, and US and UK spelling mixed. This crowd counts seconds. Sloppy copy tells them nobody checked, and checking is what we sell.
- **Performance promises.** The page promises you'll race better. A training camp can say that loosely. We can't: we show what happened, and association, not causation (PRD §4) applies to marketing too.

## 2. Why it works for this audience

**Who they are:** hybrid athletes from aspiring amateur to semi-pro. They train four to six days a week, log nearly all of it, race two to four times a year, belong to a gym or club, follow elite results and have a goal time.

| They want | HYROX answers with | OpenHealth answers with |
|---|---|---|
| To be taken seriously as athletes | "Athlete", Elite proximity, untranslated jargon | Pro-style coverage: a career page, a record book, a presser |
| Progress toward a race | Comparatives, the next race | The arc: race-to-race splits, scoped PRs, the rematch |
| Belonging | Community, social events | Followers (family, club, coach) who see what the athlete shares |
| Structure | Pillars, decks, named stations | Named dashboards and tiles in each sport's own metrics |
| Proof | Named coaches, hour counts | Every number traced to a source; "How this was calculated" on every tile |
| Fun alongside the grind | DJs, sunsets | Stories worth reading, never a box score in prose |

## 3. The OpenHealth voice

### We are / we are not

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| **Athlete-first.** The athlete is the subject and the only source of quotes. | **AI-first in tone.** No "AI-powered" headlines. The model never stars. |
| **Fluent.** Each sport in its own words: Roxzone, wall balls, Fran, RX, FTP, negative split. | **A generic fitness app.** No "workouts" when we know it was a class, no "fitness journey". |
| **Exact.** Numbers with baselines. Superlatives with scope. | **Hype.** No elite, insane, crushed it, unique, unforgettable. |
| **Pointed at the next race.** The next start line is always in view. | **A results promise.** We never claim OpenHealth makes anyone faster. |
| **Loud in the broadcast, dry at the desk.** | **One pitch everywhere.** |
| **Plain about ownership.** Your data, your call, stated up front. | **Coy about data.** No consent buried in fine print. |

### Personality

**The sports desk that covers you.** On marketing pages it's the broadcast promo: loud, proud, specific. Inside the product it's the beat reporter who knows your gym: warm, dry, specific (Playbook §4).

### Our lens

HYROX views everything through performance. We view everything through the sports desk, using the mapping in [VISION §2](VISION.md#2-what-pros-get-that-amateurs-dont): training history is the career page, benchmarks are the combine, race day is game day, the interviewer runs the presser, the dashboard is the stat sheet. Use the lens in feature names and headlines. Keep puns out of body copy and stories.

## 4. Rhythm and devices

**Borrow:**

- **Long, then short.** One sentence that lists, then a short one that lands it.
- **The countdown, filled with real numbers.** "Four races. Two years. One history." At most once per page.
- **The triad tagline.** Verb-led, every part true, once per page at most.
- **Verb-first feature names, two to four words.** Bring every app. See every split. Read your story. Share on your terms.
- **One-word names for things the athlete will say out loud** (voice first, VISION §7). Existing: Record book, Review. Candidates: Presser, Season, Rematch.
- **Both ends of the ladder.** Name the first-timer and the athlete chasing a goal time together.

**Avoid:**

- More than one adjective on a noun. Prefer a number.
- Explaining the sport to the athlete.
- Triads and fragments in the product UI and in stories. They belong to the broadcast register.

## 5. Tone by context

The voice stays the same everywhere. Energy, formality and the amount of jargon change.

| Context | Energy | Formality | Sport jargon | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing hero | High | Low | High | Identity, pro proximity, one proof | "Your training, covered like a pro's." |
| Social and promo | High | Low | High | Lead with a real number (demo data labeled as demo) | "1:28:14 to 1:14:32. Four HYROX races, every split on the record." |
| Onboarding | Medium | Low | Medium | One promise per screen; ownership stated plainly | "Where does your training live?" |
| Dashboard tiles | Low | Medium | High | Label, number, baseline. Nothing else. | "Resting HR · 3-day" |
| Spoken answers | Medium | Low | High | One sentence, built by code (D-033), number with a baseline | "Dallas was 10:23 faster than Houston in March." |
| Stories | Medium | Medium | High | Playbook §4 | — |
| Errors, consent, logistics | Low | Medium | None | What happened, what to do next. The HYROX FAQ register. | "Upload failed. Check your connection and try again." |

## 6. Applying it

### Message pillars

From the Collect, Understand, Give it back loop (VISION §4), plus ownership:

1. **Every source, one history.** Your classes, runs, watch and official race results in one place you own.
2. **See what changed.** Race against race, split against split, this block against the last.
3. **Your story, on the record.** Features built from your data and your own words.
4. **Your data, your call.** Nothing reaches followers until you approve it. Export or delete anytime.

### Tagline candidates

| Line | Note |
|---|---|
| **Your training, covered like a pro's.** | Already the onboarding heading. Identity and pro proximity in five words. **Recommended primary.** |
| Every session. Every split. One history. | The countdown device, all true |
| Train it. Race it. Read it. | Closest to HYROX's cadence; "read it" means your story |
| Your data. Your story. Your call. | Leads with ownership; good for the privacy section |

### Landing hero (draft)

> **YOUR TRAINING, COVERED LIKE A PRO'S**
>
> Pros get a stats desk, a combine and a post-race presser. You get a dozen apps that don't talk to each other. OpenHealth pulls your classes, track sessions, watch data and official HYROX splits into one history you own, then covers it the way a sports desk covers a pro: dashboards that show what happened, stories that show what changed, and every number checked against its source.
>
> Every session. Every split. One history.
>
> **[Start your history]**

Four cards, mirroring HYROX's four-benefit row:

| Card | Copy |
|---|---|
| **Bring every app** | WodUp, Mindbody, your watch and official race results. Read-only, and you can disconnect anytime. |
| **See every split** | All 16 HYROX splits laid over your heart rate, race against race. |
| **Read your story** | Features built from your own data and your own notes. No invented quotes, no made-up feelings. |
| **Share on your terms** | Followers see only what you approve. Export or delete everything anytime. |

Proof block (our version of the coach credentials, using the demo athlete and labeled as demo):

> **Henry Walsh, demo athlete.** 1:28:14 in Houston, March 2025. 1:14:32 in Dallas, September 2026. Four HYROX races and two years of classes in one history. Every number on this page comes from Henry's data, computed by code.

Ladder line: "Your first HYROX or your tenth, your coverage starts with what you've already logged."

Truth is our edge over a page like HYROX's. Where they write "unforgettable", we show the split.

### Word list

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| athlete | user |
| class, run, race, WOD, ride (when known) | workout |
| your history, your season | fitness journey |
| see, get | unlock, level up, supercharge |
| the number, with a baseline | elite, insane, beast mode, crushed it |
| a specific fact | unique, unforgettable, iconic, world-class |
| what the athlete gets | AI-powered (in headlines) |
| what happened, what changed | insights, optimize |
| was followed by, lined up with (Playbook §4) | because, led to, paid off |

**Never:** promise that OpenHealth will make an athlete faster or fitter; write a testimonial or quote nobody said; print an unscoped "your best ever".

### Checklist before any copy ships

1. Does it call them an athlete and use their sport's own words?
2. Is a real number, with a baseline, doing the proving?
3. Is every superlative scoped?
4. Does it point at the next race or the next session?
5. Does it claim a cause? Rewrite it.
6. Can someone say it out loud in one breath?
7. Is it spelled right, in US English?

## 7. Open questions

Each has a recommendation; Kevin confirms or overrides.

1. **Spelling.** Recommend US English throughout (Austin-based founder, US launch audience).
2. **Performance promises in marketing.** Recommend none: promise coverage and understanding, not results. HYROX can say "race better"; we'd be claiming a cause we can't show.
3. **How loud the broadcast gets.** Recommend all-caps heroes and one triad per page on marketing pages only. Product UI stays sentence case and plain.
4. **Primary tagline.** Recommend keeping "Your training, covered like a pro's."
5. **Sports-desk names for features** (Presser, Record book, Season review). Recommend yes for named features, no puns in microcopy or stories.
6. **The AI estimate label.** The timeline says "Blocks estimated by AI · not exact". Human first argues for "Blocks estimated · tap to adjust", with "matched by AI" moved into "How this was calculated". Honesty argues for keeping AI in the label. Recommend the shorter label plus the calc note; it's Kevin's call.
